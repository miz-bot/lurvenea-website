ingredients:
  - "1 cup almond flour (blanched, fine)"
  - "3/4 cup oat flour (or rolled oats blended fine)"
  - "1/2 tsp baking soda"
  - "1/4 tsp fine sea salt"
  - "1/4 cup butter or coconut oil, melted and slightly cooled"
  - "1/3 cup coconut sugar"
  - "3 tbsp pure maple syrup"
  - "1 large egg, room temperature"
  - "1 tsp vanilla extract"
  - "3/4 cup dark chocolate chips or chopped chocolate (70%+)"
steps:
  - "Preheat oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment."
  - "Whisk together the almond flour, oat flour, baking soda, and salt until evenly combined with no lumps."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the melted butter, coconut sugar, and maple syrup until smooth, then whisk in the egg and vanilla until the mixture looks glossy and slightly thickened — about 30 seconds of brisk whisking."
  - "Pour the wet mixture into the dry and stir until just combined; stop as soon as no dry flour remains."
  - "Fold in the chocolate by hand."
  - "Chill the dough for 10 minutes — this keeps the cookies thick and chewy instead of spreading thin."
  - "Scoop into 18 balls, space them on the sheet, and flatten slightly."
  - "Bake 10–12 minutes, until the edges are set and golden but the centers still look soft. Cool on the sheet 5 minutes before moving — they finish setting as they cool."
whyItWorks:
  - "Almond flour keeps the cookies tender and moist; oat flour gives structure so they hold together instead of crumbling — the blend is what makes them feel like a real cookie."
  - "Coconut sugar and maple syrup brown beautifully and add a caramel note, so you get depth without refined white sugar."
  - "Melting the butter instead of creaming it means the whole dough comes together with just a whisk and a spoon — no electric mixer. Whisking the egg into the wet mix until glossy is what gives the cookies an even, tender crumb."
  - "Chilling firms the fat so the cookies stay thick and chewy rather than spreading flat."
substitutions:
  - "Vegan: use a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water, rested 5 minutes) and coconut oil instead of butter."
  - "Nut-free: replace the almond flour with an extra 3/4 cup oat flour plus 2 tbsp coconut flour, and add 1 tbsp more maple syrup (coconut flour is drying)."
  - "Lower sugar: reduce the coconut sugar to 1/4 cup; the maple keeps them from tasting flat."
  - "Gluten-free: use certified gluten-free oats for the oat flour — everything else is already GF."
tips:
  - "Don't overbake — pull them when the centers still look slightly underdone. They set on the tray, and that's what keeps them chewy."
  - "Press a few extra chocolate pieces onto each dough ball before baking for the bakery look."
  - "Use a room-temperature egg; a cold egg can seize the melted butter into little clumps."
  - "Store in an airtight container up to 5 days, or freeze the raw scooped dough and bake from frozen (add 1–2 minutes)."

These healthy chocolate chip cookies are soft, chewy, and made with better-for-you ingredients — almond and oat flour instead of refined white flour, coconut sugar and a little maple syrup instead of white sugar, and plenty of dark chocolate. No mixer required: you whisk the melted butter and sugars smooth, whisk in the egg until glossy, then stir the flours in by hand. They bake up crisp at the edges, tender in the middle, and stay soft for days.
