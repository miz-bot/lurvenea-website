ingredients:
  - "1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled"
  - "1/2 cup brown sugar"
  - "1/4 cup granulated sugar"
  - "1 large egg plus 1 yolk, room temperature"
  - "1 tsp vanilla extract"
  - "1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour"
  - "1/2 tsp baking soda"
  - "1/4 tsp fine sea salt"
  - "3/4 cup chocolate chips or chopped chocolate"
steps:
  - "Preheat oven to 350F (175C) and line a baking sheet with parchment."
  - "Whisk the melted butter with both sugars until smooth and glossy."
  - "Whisk in the egg, yolk, and vanilla until silky."
  - "Add the flour, baking soda, and salt, and stir with a spatula just until no dry flour remains."
  - "Fold in the chocolate."
  - "Scoop into 18 tall balls (taller than wide keeps them thick) and space on the sheet."
  - "Bake 9-11 minutes, until edges are set but centers still look soft and underdone."
  - "Cool on the sheet 5 minutes before moving."
whyItWorks:
  - "Melted butter and an extra yolk add moisture and give that dense, chewy texture instead of a cakey one."
  - "More brown sugar than white keeps them soft and adds a caramel note."
  - "Whisking the butter, sugar, and egg until glossy means no mixer needed."
  - "Scooping the dough tall and pulling them underdone locks in thick, chewy centers."
substitutions:
  - "Extra gooey: use chopped chocolate bars instead of chips for melty pools."
  - "Brown butter: brown the butter first for deeper flavor."
  - "Gluten-free: use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour."
  - "Add nuts: fold in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts."
tips:
  - "Don't overbake; they firm up as they cool and stay chewy."
  - "Chilling the dough 30 minutes makes them even thicker (optional)."
  - "Press extra chocolate on top of each ball before baking."
  - "Store airtight up to 5 days with a slice of bread to keep them soft."

These chewy chocolate chip cookies are thick, soft in the middle, and loaded with gooey pools of melty chocolate — and they come together in one bowl with just a whisk, no mixer. The secret to that bakery-style chew is melted butter, an extra egg yolk, and pulling them from the oven while the centers still look underdone.
